# Pivot usefulness audit, November 2026

Audit date: 2026-11-02.
Audited commit: `origin/main` at `6f93a1d` (fix(deps): Next.js 16.4.3 for critical security advisories).
Also reviewed: the `production-hardening` branch (draft PR #1) and three local commits on top of it that are not on GitHub.
Screenshots: `docs/audit-2026-11/` (desktop at 1440x900, phone at 390x844, full-page captures).

## Summary

Pivot is a polished, calm, well-tested "pause button" for everyday urges, and the core loop works end to end.
It is already deployed and publicly reachable at `https://pivot-lilac-nu.vercel.app`, but that deployment runs `main`, which lacks the 17 production-hardening commits on GitHub (20 locally) that sit on an unmerged branch.
The live site has no privacy policy or terms, sends free-text urge notes to Gemini, and its crisis matcher misses common phrasings such as "I feel suicidal" and "I want to die".
The recommendation step, which is the product's main promise, is currently a fixed ordering that ignores the tags, intensity, and note the user just entered.

Verdict: landing page plus waitlist on n8builds.dev now, not a live-build listing.
The idea and the craft are good enough to collect interest, but the live app should not be promoted until the safety and legal gaps below are closed and the hardened branch is merged.

## What it is

Pivot is a mobile-first web app for the moment an urge appears, such as late-night scrolling, checking email, or snacking.
The user sets up one or more "pivots" (a habit to move away from), each with its own approved replacement actions.
When an urge hits, the user taps "I'm having an urge", optionally adds context (tags, a one-time note, intensity), gets one recommended replacement with a first step and a timer, and checks in afterwards.
History shows the "redirected moments", and recommendations re-rank based on what the user said helped.

Design principles visible in the product and the README:

- No streaks, scores, or judgmental language.
- Anonymous first, with a secure session cookie and optional GitHub sign-in to sync devices.
- Free-text context is used once and never stored.
- High-risk and clinical-category language routes to crisis or specialized support instead of a habit suggestion.
- Full JSON export and complete deletion.

Stack: Next.js 16 App Router, React 19, Tailwind 4, Turso/libSQL (SQLite locally), Auth.js with GitHub, optional Gemini `gemini-3.1-flash-lite`, Vitest, and Playwright.

## Who it is for

The natural audience is adults who want to change a non-clinical everyday habit without the shame mechanics of streak counters.
Examples are doomscrolling, compulsive checking, procrastination loops, snacking out of boredom, and nail biting.
It is explicitly not for addiction recovery, eating disorders, or crisis care, and it says so.

One mismatch stands out: the only account option is GitHub sign-in, which fits developers but not the general audience the copy is written for.
For most users "sync across devices" will mean email magic links or passkeys.

## Market and differentiation

The space is crowded at the simple end.
2026 App Store listings include many urge-surfing timers and trackers: Craving Holdout, Ride The Urge, Urge Surfer, Urge Jar, Harness, Waveguard, Sober: Quit Anything, and several apps literally called "Pause".
Most are a breathing timer plus a streak or sobriety counter, iOS-first, and focused on a single vice (sugar, porn, alcohol, nicotine).
Adjacent products include one sec (a friction screen before opening an app, with a PNAS study reporting a 57% drop in target-app openings), Opal and ScreenZen for screen time, and clinically grounded programs such as Judson Brewer's Craving to Quit and Unwinding Anxiety, plus I Am Sober and Quitzilla for counters.

What can make Pivot distinct:

1. Replacement, not resistance: most competitors ask you to wait out the urge; Pivot hands you a specific next action with a first step and a timer, drawn from a list you chose. That is the strongest idea here and should lead the pitch.
2. No streaks, no shame: competitors almost universally use streak counters that punish lapses; Pivot's "small moments count" stance is a real point of difference for people who have bounced off streak apps.
3. Any habit, many pivots, each with its own toolkit, rather than a single-vice app.
4. Privacy by default: no account, notes never stored, export and delete in one place, and a web app that needs no install.
5. Learning what helps: if recommendations actually adapt to context and outcomes, Pivot can say "for you, when tired, tea works better than a walk", which none of the timer apps do. Today this is the weakest part, so it is the highest-leverage engineering investment.

Weaknesses against the field: no native app (the PWA work helps on Android only), no way to intercept the trigger itself the way one sec does, and no evidence base or clinical framing.

Sensitivity: this product touches urges, lapses, and sometimes crisis moments.
Tone is already excellent.
The priorities are a robust crisis route, honest data disclosures (especially any AI processing of notes), an adults-only stance until a guardian flow exists, and never adding growth mechanics that shame lapses.

## Verdict

Landing page plus waitlist on n8builds.dev now; do not list it as a live build yet; do not park it.

Why not park: the concept is clear, the design and tone are strong, the code is healthy, and there is a real differentiation story (replacement over resistance, no streaks, privacy).
Why not a live build: the deployed app has a crisis-routing gap and no privacy policy while sending notes to an AI model, its phone urge screen hides the main action below the fold, and the recommendation does not yet deliver on "a better fit".

Minimum work before the landing page and waitlist go up:

1. Fix the crisis matcher and make Gemini `review` or `blocked` results route to support (problem 1), on `main` and on the hardened branch.
2. Either take the current production deployment private or behind an invite, or publish privacy, terms, and safety pages and disable Gemini on production until AI processing is disclosed (problem 2), because a public landing page will send people to whatever is reachable.
3. Write the landing copy around "replacement, not resistance" and "no streaks", with a clear not-for-crisis line and a link to 988.

Minimum work before listing it as a live build:

1. Merge and deploy `production-hardening` (problem 5), with the local commits pushed and the hourly cron resolved.
2. Phone fold fix for the urge screen (problem 3).
3. Context-aware deterministic ranking and human reasons (problem 4).
4. A non-GitHub sync option, or drop sync from the pitch (problem 8).
5. Onboarding and first-visit clarity (problems 6 and 7).
